# Brand tracker: weighting, banner tables and significance testing

A consumer brand runs a quarterly brand-health survey. Four waves were fielded in 2025, with about 1,500 US adults per wave. The insight team has two jobs each quarter: tell leadership whether the brand moved, and hand the client a weighted banner table plus an SPSS file. This example does both with Pulse, from generating the data to producing the `.sav` file.

The data is synthetic and was generated by Pulse from a spec with a fixed seed. Because we built it, we know which movements are real and which are noise. That lets us check each analytical choice against the truth.

## The business question

1. **Did the brand move in 2025?** We track awareness (unaided and aided), consideration, purchase intent (top-2-box) and Net Promoter Score (NPS), and say which wave-over-wave changes are real.
2. **Who is the brand strong with right now?** This is the Q4 banner: KPIs and brand-image attributes by gender, age and region, with significance letters.
3. **What can we tell the client, and what can we not?** Some of the answers change depending on whether the data is weighted and whether the tests are corrected for multiple comparisons.

## Headline findings

| # | Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The unaided-awareness "gain" is a sampling artefact.** Unweighted, unaided awareness rises from 32.3% in Q1 to 38.3% in Q4 (p < 0.001). Weighted to census, it goes from 28.8% to 28.4% (p = 0.865). Q4 fieldwork over-delivered 18–34s: they were 59.3% of the Q4 sample against a census share of 29%. | Steps 0–3 |
| 2 | **Weighting flips the sign of NPS.** Unweighted NPS is +5.6 (95% CI +3.3 to +8.0). Weighted NPS is −3.5 (95% CI −6.1 to −0.8). Neither interval contains zero, so the two answers really do disagree. The cause is the same young skew: 18–34s score +28, while people aged 55 and over score −29. | Step 4 |
| 3 | **Consideration really did grow.** Among aware adults, weighted consideration rose from 34.3% in Q1 to 51.9% in Q4. The Q3→Q4 step of +8.5 points survives a Holm correction across all 15 tracker tests (adjusted p = 0.006). The Q2→Q3 step of +6.1 points is significant uncorrected (p = 0.004) but just misses after correction (0.058). | Step 5 |
| 4 | **Multiple-comparison correction removes the false alarms.** Uncorrected, the tracker reports Q4 dips in aided awareness (−4.2 points, p = 0.021) and unaided awareness (−4.0 points, p = 0.046). The Q4 banner reports a West-over-Midwest lead on top-2-box intent (37.7% vs 27.8%, p = 0.047). All three are noise in the generating model, and none survives Holm (adjusted p = 0.278, 0.552 and 1.000). | Steps 5–6 |
| 5 | **"Eco-friendly" is the image story.** Association with "Eco-friendly" doubled, from 12.3% in Q1 to 24.2% in Q4 among aware adults. Every quarter, people who make that association are more likely to consider the brand: 58.8% vs 49.8% in Q4. | Steps 8–9 |

The practical implication: report the brand as **stable on awareness and NPS (slightly negative), and growing on consideration, with the eco message as the most likely driver.** The unweighted deck would have reported an awareness win and a positive NPS. Both are wrong.

## Dataset

`spec/brand_tracker.synth.json` holds the generating spec. `pulse synth from-schema --seed 2025` turns it into `brand_tracker_sample.pulse`: 6,000 respondents and 11 fields, with no weight. `pulse rake` then writes `brand_tracker.pulse`, the same records plus a `weight` column (Step 0). Every request reads `brand_tracker.pulse`.

| Field | Type | Content |
|---|---|---|
| `resp_id` | `u32` | Respondent ID, 100001… |
| `wave` | `categorical_u8` | `W1`…`W4` (label table `wave_names` → "2025 Q1"…"2025 Q4") |
| `age_band` | `categorical_u8` | `18-34`, `35-54`, `55+` (label table `age_names`) |
| `gender` | `categorical_u8` | `Female`, `Male` (label table `gender_names`) |
| `region` | `categorical_u8` | `NE`, `MW`, `SO`, `WE` (label table `region_names`) |
| `aware_unaided` | `packed_bool` | Named the brand unprompted |
| `aware_aided` | `packed_bool` | Recognised the brand from a list |
| `consider` | `packed_bool`, nullable | Would consider; asked only if aided-aware |
| `intent` | `u8`, nullable | Purchase intent 1–5; asked only if aided-aware |
| `nps` | `u8`, nullable | Likelihood to recommend 0–10; asked only if aided-aware |
| `attributes` | `set_u8`, nullable | Multi-select brand image: Good value, High quality, Innovative, Trustworthy, Stylish, Eco-friendly, For people like me, None of these; asked only if aided-aware |
| `weight` | `f64` | Raking weight to census age, gender and region within each wave; added by `pulse rake` |

### Structure and ground truth built into the spec

**The sample is skewed young, and Q4 is worse.** A `categorical_pairs` entry draws `age_band` conditional on `wave`. In Q1–Q3 the draw is 40% / 36% / 24% (18–34 / 35–54 / 55+). In Q4 it is 58% / 28% / 14%. Census targets are 29% / 33% / 38%. Women (55% of the sample vs 51% census) and the Northeast are also over-represented.

**The spec carries no weight.** Pulse computes the weights from the census targets after the data exists, the way a fieldwork agency would (Step 0).

**Each KPI has a planted signal and noise.** Each KPI is a synth `models` entry: an ordered-probit shift by age band and, for consideration only, by wave and by the Eco-friendly image bit.

| KPI | What drives it |
|---|---|
| Unaided and aided awareness | Strongly age-driven; **flat across waves** |
| Consideration | Age, plus a **real lift in Q3 and a larger one in Q4** (a campaign), plus a boost for respondents who see the brand as Eco-friendly |
| Purchase intent | Mildly age-driven; flat across waves and regions |
| NPS | Strongly age-driven (young promoters, older detractors); flat across waves and regions |

**Brand image (multi-select).** `set_bernoulli` draws each option, and `set_categorical_pairs` makes some options depend on other fields:

| Option | Depends on |
|---|---|
| Innovative, Stylish, For people like me | Age (young higher) |
| Eco-friendly | Wave: 12% → 13% → 19% → 24% |
| Trustworthy | Region: Midwest 42% vs 35% elsewhere |

**Question routing.** Structural rules make unaided awareness imply aided awareness. They also null consideration, intent, NPS and image for the not-aware, and set "None of these" when nothing else was ticked.

## Method

- **Weights.** `pulse rake` rakes each wave separately to the same census margins (age, gender, region) and writes the result as the `weight` column. Every figure marked "weighted" uses `{"weight": {"field": "weight", "kind": "probability"}}` on the request. Weighted inference uses the Kish effective sample size, so the tests account for the variance the weights add. Where a step compares weighted and unweighted figures side by side in one request, the unweighted slots opt out with `"weight": null`.
- **Bases.** Awareness uses all adults. Consideration, top-2-box intent, NPS and image use the aided-aware: those questions were only asked of them. Top-2-box is intent codes 4 and 5. NPS is the mean of a score that is +100 for 9–10, −100 for 0–6 and 0 otherwise, so the mean *is* the NPS.
- **Significance.** Wave-over-wave changes use the `*_VS_PRIOR` overlays, which test each wave against the one before it. Each banner table is one Compose `banner` request, and Pulse returns the significance letters (`params.letters` on the pairwise overlay). All tests are two-sided at α = 0.05. Corrections use a `multiplicity` block. `family: "request"` on a single request, and `family: "compose"` on a banner, pool every test in that request or table into one family.

Every step below is one file in `req/`. Run it with `pulse api process` (a single request) or `pulse api compose` (a `requests` batch or a banner), after `pulse api predict` / `predict-compose`. `PULSE_LABEL_TABLES_DIR=./labels` registers the label tables. `run.sh` runs all of it, and `tables.sh` writes every table on this page from the responses it leaves in `out/`.

## Walkthrough

### Step 0: Generate the sample and compute the weights

```sh
pulse synth from-schema -s spec/brand_tracker.synth.json -o brand_tracker_sample.pulse --seed 2025 --json
pulse rake brand_tracker_sample.pulse -o brand_tracker.pulse --margins spec/census_targets.json \
  --by wave --weight-field weight --json
pulse cohort inspect brand_tracker.pulse --json
```

The synth run returned `rows_generated: 6000` and `rows_rejected: 0`, with no warnings. The same seed produces a byte-identical file.

### Step 3: Is the unaided gain real? (`req/03_unaided_phantom_gain.json`)

On the weighted slot the test reads each cell's Kish `n_eff`.

Unweighted, there are three "significant" gains on unaided awareness, including a 6-point year-on-year rise. Weighted, the year-on-year comparison is flat (p = 0.865). ### Step 5: The KPI tracker: wave-over-wave tests, corrected across all KPIs (`req/05_kpi_tracker.json`)

The whole tracker is one grouped Process by wave. Each KPI is an aggregation, and each has two overlays:

- `OVERLAY_DELTA_VS_PRIOR` gives the change against the previous wave in the KPI's own units.
- An inferential `*_VS_PRIOR` overlay tests that change. Proportions use `OVERLAY_PROP_Z_VS_PRIOR` on the mean of a 0/1 indicator. NPS uses `OVERLAY_Z_VS_PRIOR`, which reads the mean, variance and n of an `AGG_VARIANCE` slot on the same score.

`params.field` points each overlay at its KPI. A request-level `"multiplicity": {"method": "holm", "family": "request"}` pools every test in the request (5 KPIs × 3 wave steps). Each test then carries `p_adjusted` beside the untouched raw `p_value`.

The bases come from the data rather than from filters. Consideration is null for the not-aware, and `AGG_AVERAGE` skips nulls. The top-2-box and NPS formulas return null when the answer is null (`intent == nil ? nil : ...`), so their means are of the aware too.

```json
{"cohort": {"filename": "brand_tracker.pulse", "data_dir": "."},
 "weight": {"field": "weight", "kind": "probability"},
 "attributes": [
   {"type": "ATTR_FORMULA", "field": "intent", "label": "intent_t2b",
    "expression": "intent == nil ? nil : (intent >= 4 ? 1 : 0)"},
   {"type": "ATTR_FORMULA", "field": "nps", "label": "nps_score",
    "expression": "nps == nil ? nil : (nps >= 9 ? 100 : (nps <= 6 ? -100 : 0))"}],
 "groups": [{"type": "GROUP_CATEGORY", "field": "wave"}],
 "aggregations": [
   {"type": "AGG_AVERAGE", "field": "consider", "label": "consider"},
   {"type": "AGG_AVERAGE", "field": "nps_score", "label": "nps"},
   {"type": "AGG_VARIANCE", "field": "nps_score", "label": "nps_variance"},
   "... aided, unaided, intent_t2b ..."],
 "overlays": [
   {"name": "consider_change", "kind": "OVERLAY_DELTA_VS_PRIOR", "scope": "group", "params": {"field": "consider"}},
   {"name": "consider_test", "kind": "OVERLAY_PROP_Z_VS_PRIOR", "scope": "group", "params": {"field": "consider"}},
   {"name": "nps_change", "kind": "OVERLAY_DELTA_VS_PRIOR", "scope": "group", "params": {"field": "nps"}},
   {"name": "nps_test", "kind": "OVERLAY_Z_VS_PRIOR", "scope": "group", "params": {"field": "nps_variance"}},
   "... the same pair for aided, unaided and intent_t2b ..."],
 "multiplicity": {"method": "holm", "family": "request"}}
```

All figures are weighted.

Read against the ground truth:

- **The Q4 dips in awareness are false positives.** Aided (p = 0.021) and unaided (p = 0.046) are both significant uncorrected, and the spec has no wave effect on awareness. Holm removes both (0.278 and 0.552).
- **The Q3→Q4 consideration lift is real and survives correction** (adjusted p = 0.006).
- **The Q2→Q3 consideration step is also real.** The spec plants a Q3 lift, but Holm (0.058) cannot quite confirm it with these sample sizes. Correction protects against false alarms at the cost of power.

The tests assume each wave is a fresh sample, which is true of this tracker. A panel that re-interviews the same people would need a paired test instead.

### Step 6: The Q4 banner with significance letters (`req/06_q4_banner.json`)

The whole banner is one Compose request with a `banner` block. It uses Q4 interviews only and is weighted throughout.

- `request` is the base every slot starts from: cohort, weight, the Q4 filter, the two derived scores, and a crosstab whose single row is the wave.
- `sections` are the stub rows. Each has its own base and lists its KPIs as `cells` (several measures stacked as stub rows). The aware section adds the aided-aware filter.
- `groups` are the banner columns: gender, age band and region. `total: true` adds the Total column.
- `overlays` go on every group slot. `params.letters` asks Pulse for the significance letters.

Pulse expands this into eight ordinary crosstab slots (two sections × Total and three groups) and stitches them back into one table in `banner.sections`, with the letters on the stitched coordinates. Letters run on across the banner (Gender A–B, Age C–E, Region F–I), but a column is only ever compared with columns of its own group. The Total column is compared with nothing.

```json
{"requests": [],
 "multiplicity": {"method": "holm", "family": "compose"},
 "banner": {
   "request": {"cohort": {"filename": "brand_tracker.pulse", "data_dir": "."},
               "weight": {"field": "weight", "kind": "probability"},
               "filterers": [{"type": "FILTER_INCLUDE", "field": "wave", "values": ["W4"]}],
               "attributes": ["... intent_t2b (ATTR_CODE_IN codes 4, 5) and nps_score (ATTR_FORMULA) ..."],
               "crosstab": {"rows": [{"type": "GROUP_CATEGORY", "field": "wave"}],
```

### Step 10: Client hand-off: labelled SPSS file, labelled Excel, and a read-back check

```sh
export PULSE_LABEL_TABLES_DIR=./labels
pulse export predict -i brand_tracker.pulse --format spss --sanitize-names --json
pulse export spss    -i brand_tracker.pulse -o deliver/brand_tracker_2025.sav --sanitize-names \
      --labels wave=wave_names --labels age_band=age_names --labels gender=gender_names \
      --labels region=region_names --json
pulse export excel   -i brand_tracker.pulse -o deliver/brand_tracker_2025.xlsx \
      --labels wave=wave_names:augment --labels region=region_names:augment --json
pulse import spss    -i deliver/brand_tracker_2025.sav -o deliver/roundtrip.pulse --json
pulse api process    -r req/12_roundtrip_check.json --json
```

- **Export predict and the export.** Without `--sanitize-names`, export predict refuses with `PULSE_SPSS_NAME_INVALID`: multi-select options become SPSS indicator variables named after the option, and "Good value" is not a legal SPSS name. With the flag, the export writes 6,000 cases. It reports two warnings:
  - `PULSE_SPSS_NAME_SANITIZED` lists the five renames, such as `Good value` → `Good_value`.
  - `PULSE_SPSS_SIDECAR_ABSENT` is expected, because this cohort never came from SPSS.
- **What the `.sav` contains.** Each field description becomes the SPSS variable label. The four `--labels` bindings turn wave, age band, gender and region into numeric variables with value labels, so the client sees "2025 Q4", "55 and over" and "Northeast" in SPSS, not bare codes. The codes number the categories in the order they first appear in the cohort, so wave code 1 is "2025 Q3" (the first respondent's wave); the value label is what to read. `attributes` becomes eight 0/1 dichotomies plus a multiple-dichotomy set definition, so it re-imports as a multi-select. `weight` is carried as a variable, and the client applies it with `WEIGHT BY weight`.
- **The Excel copy.** It keeps the codes and adds `wave_label` ("2025 Q1"…) and `region_label` ("Northeast"…) columns from the label tables.
- **Read-back check.** Importing the `.sav` gives back the codes, and the metadata sidecar (`deliver/roundtrip.pulse.spss.json`) carries all 13 value labels. `req/12_roundtrip_check.json` is the Step 2 request pointed at the re-imported cohort, grouped by the SPSS wave code. It returns exactly the Step 2 figures: for code 2 ("2025 Q4"), weighted unaided awareness 28.4% and weighted consideration 51.9%. So the client's file reproduces the deck.

## Implications for the brand team

- **Do not claim an awareness win for 2025.** Weighted unaided awareness is flat at 28–32%. The apparent gain is Q4's young-skewed sample.
- **NPS is slightly negative (−3.5), not positive.** It is polarised by age: +28 among 18–34s and −29 among people aged 55 and over. Any NPS target should be set and tracked on the weighted figure, by age band.
- **Consideration is the real 2025 story:** +17.7 points among the aware, with the Q4 step confirmed after correction. The Eco-friendly image gain tracks it closely.
- **Fix Q4 fieldwork quotas.** Raking recovered the census mix, but at 60% efficiency. Q4 has the precision of 889 interviews, which is why some genuine Q4 differences cannot be confirmed.
- **Agree a correction policy with the client.** This example uses Holm for headline claims and BH for banner screening. Without one, about 1 in 20 null comparisons will show up as a letter.
