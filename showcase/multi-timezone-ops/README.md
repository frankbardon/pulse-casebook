# Multi-time-zone delivery operations

A logistics network with six warehouses in six time zones stores every delivery as a UTC instant. That is the right way to store time, and the wrong way to read it for operations. This walkthrough shows what changes when Pulse reads each warehouse on its own local calendar and clock. It covers daily volumes and on-time rates, incident detection, trend testing, peak-hour staffing, and two overnight feeds that arrive as naive local times across a daylight-saving change.

Every number below was returned by a Pulse request in [`req/`](req/) or by a `pulse` CLI command shown in the step. To re-run everything and check the figures, see [REPRODUCE.md](REPRODUCE.md).

> **Pulse version.** The delivery cohort is generated by `pulse synth from-schema` with zone-aware `datetime` fields, rule draws and time-effect rule functions. These arrive in the Pulse alpha after v1.0.0-alpha.33 (the synth-realism work, roadmap U52). The casebook `Makefile` still pins alpha.33; build Pulse from a checkout that has U52 until the pin moves (see [REPRODUCE.md](REPRODUCE.md)).

## The business question

> *For each warehouse, by its own business day: how many parcels did we deliver, how many were on time, when do the peaks fall, and did anything go wrong?*

Three operational decisions depend on the answer:

1. **Incident response.** Which warehouse had a bad day, and which day was it?
2. **Performance reviews.** Is any site improving or slipping, or is it just noise?
3. **Staffing.** In which local hours does each dock need the most drivers, before and after the clocks change?

## Dataset

**Fictional network.** Six warehouses. Each is open Monday–Saturday, 07:00–20:59 local time, and closed on Sundays.

| Code | City | Zone | DST change inside the window |
|---|---|---|---|
| LAX | Los Angeles | `America/Los_Angeles` | spring forward 2026-03-08 |
| ORD | Chicago | `America/Chicago` | spring forward 2026-03-08 |
| NYC | New York | `America/New_York` | spring forward 2026-03-08 |
| LON | London | `Europe/London` | spring forward 2026-03-29 |
| BLR | Bengaluru | `Asia/Kolkata` | none (UTC+05:30 all year) |
| SYD | Sydney | `Australia/Sydney` | **fall back** 2026-04-05 (southern hemisphere) |

**Window.** 26 full local weeks, Monday 2026-02-02 to Sunday 2026-08-02.

**`deliveries.pulse`: 17,160 deliveries** (one row per hand-over), generated by Pulse from [`data/deliveries.synth.json`](data/deliveries.synth.json) with seed `20260202`.

| Field | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `delivery_id` | `u32` | Unique id |
| `delivered_at` | `datetime` | Hand-over instant, UTC (`2026-02-01T21:22:18Z`) |
| `warehouse` | `categorical_u8` | LAX, ORD, NYC, LON, BLR, SYD |
| `zone` | `categorical_u8` | IANA zone of the warehouse |
| `route` | `categorical_u8` | 8 routes per site (`SYD-R4`) |
| `service_level` | `categorical_u8` | standard / express |
| `duration_min` | `f64` | Minutes from van departure to hand-over |
| `on_time` | `packed_bool` | Handed over inside the promised window |
| `weight_kg` | `f64` | Billed weight |

**`data/feeds/*.csv`: overnight sorter batches** (committed import fixtures: a naive wall-clock string that repeats or skips an hour is what a feed sends, not something a generator of instants produces) → [`schemas/night_sort.schema.json`](schemas/night_sort.schema.json). Each hub's sorter closes a batch every 15 minutes between 00:00 and 05:59 local. It logs the time as a **naive local wall-clock string** (`2026-04-05 02:15:00`), with no offset.

| File | Rows | Nights | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| `syd_night_sort.csv` | 172 | 2026-04-02 … 04-08 | The fall-back night repeats 02:00–02:59, so the sorter really ran 28 batches and wrote `02:00`, `02:15`, `02:30` and `02:45` twice |
| `nyc_night_sort.csv` | 168 | 2026-03-05 … 03-11 | A legacy controller stamps batches with schedule slots that ignore DST, so on the spring-forward night it writes four `02:xx` times that never existed in New York |

**Built-in ground truth.** The dataset is synthetic and deterministic, with the following facts planted in it for the analysis to recover:

- Every site is **closed on local Sundays**. A weekday averages 20 deliveries per site and a Saturday 10.
- **SYD sortation outage on Sydney days Tue 2026-05-12 to Thu 2026-05-14.** On-time probability drops to 0.45 and durations stretch by 1.8×.
- **BLR improves steadily.** Its base on-time probability rises linearly from 0.80 to 0.95 over the 26 weeks.
- **LON is flat at 0.91.** The other sites are also flat (0.90–0.93). All sites have day-level noise.
- Each site has its own local hour-of-day profile. For example, SYD is busiest early in the morning and BLR has morning and evening peaks.

**How the spec plants it.** [`data/deliveries.synth.json`](data/deliveries.synth.json) is the whole generator; `run_all.sh` runs it with `pulse synth from-schema`. `delivered_at` is a `uniform_datetime` read in each row's own `zone` (`time_zone_field`), and six `"output": false` helper fields draw a hand-over time on each warehouse's own clock, with that site's 24 `hour_weights` and the `weekday_weights` `[1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 0.5, 0]` (Monday first, so Saturday is half a day and Sunday is closed). A rule copies the row's site helper into `delivered_at`; the helpers never reach the file. The on-time outcome is drawn against a planted probability with the time-effect rule functions, which read `delivered_at` on the row's local clock:

```json
{"set_expr": {"p_on_time": "(warehouse == \"BLR\" ? 0.80 + trend(delivered_at, 0.15 / 182) : warehouse == \"LAX\" ? 0.91 : … : 0.93) + day_noise"}},
{"when": "warehouse == \"SYD\" && between(delivered_at, \"2026-05-12\", \"2026-05-14\")", "set": {"p_on_time": 0.45}},
{"set_expr": {"on_time": "bernoulli(min(1, max(0, p_on_time)))"}}
```

`trend(delivered_at, 0.15 / 182)` adds 0.15 over the 182 local days from Monday 2026-02-02; `between(…)` covers the three Sydney days of the outage, which also multiplies `duration_min` by 1.8. The day-level noise is a fixed pattern of ±3 points per site and local day, a hash of the day number (`floor(trend(delivered_at, 1))`) built from `sin`, so it needs no random draw. Volumes are random draws, so a weekday carries 20 deliveries on average and typically 12–28.

## Methodology

- **Store UTC, read local.** Every request names the zone it reads time in. `time_zone` sets one zone for the whole request; a slot-level `tz` overrides it for one filter or grouper. Pulse then applies that zone to the local calendar day, ISO week, hour of day and date-range boundaries, including across DST changes.
- **One request for the whole network.** Each delivery row carries its warehouse's IANA zone in the categorical `zone` field. With `"time_zone_field": "zone"`, every date grouper, date-range filter, date part and date feature reads each row in that row's own zone. A single request can therefore group by `warehouse` and still read every site on its own calendar. The UTC contrasts set `"time_zone": "UTC"`, and the single-site drill-downs set `time_zone` to that site's zone.
- **Daily series.** Each daily series is `GROUP_DATE` (`component: day`) plus `AGG_AVERAGE` over the boolean `on_time`, which gives the on-time rate. Window functions add smoothing and day-over-day change. Rolling overlays score each day against the 28 operating days before it.
- **Trend testing.** Trends are tested with Mann-Kendall (`TEST_TREND`) on **weekly** on-time rates, with a Holm correction across the six warehouses.
- **Feed validation.** Naive-time feeds are imported with `--source-tz`. Pulse refuses DST-ambiguous or nonexistent times unless a `--dst-policy` is chosen.

### Step 3: Flag the incident automatically

**Request: daily screen.** [`req/05_daily_anomaly_screen.json`](req/05_daily_anomaly_screen.json) screens the whole network in one request. It groups by `[warehouse, day]` with every day read in the warehouse's own zone, drops local Saturdays, and decorates the series with two rolling overlays. Both overlays score the on-time rate (`params.field`) against the **28 preceding operating days**:

```json
{
  "cohort": {"filename": "deliveries.pulse", "data_dir": "."},
  "time_zone_field": "zone",
  "features": [{"type": "FEAT_DATE_FEATURES", "field": "delivered_at", "label": "local"}],
  "filterers": [{"type": "FILTER_EXCLUDE", "field": "local_dow", "values": ["6"]}],
  "groups": [
    {"type": "GROUP_CATEGORY", "field": "warehouse"},
    {"type": "GROUP_DATE", "field": "delivered_at", "params": {"component": "day"}}
  ],
  "aggregations": [
    {"type": "AGG_AVERAGE", "field": "on_time", "label": "on_time_rate"},
    {"type": "AGG_COUNT", "field": "delivery_id", "label": "deliveries"}
  ],
  "overlays": [
    {"name": "on_time_rolling_z", "kind": "OVERLAY_ZSCORE_VS_ROLLING", "scope": "group",
     "ref": {"rolling_mean": {}}, "params": {"window": 28, "min_periods": 28, "field": "on_time_rate"}},
    {"name": "on_time_rolling_index", "kind": "OVERLAY_INDEX_VS_ROLLING_MEAN", "scope": "group",
     "ref": {"rolling_mean": {}}, "params": {"window": 28, "min_periods": 28, "field": "on_time_rate"}}
  ],
  "sort": [{"field": "warehouse"}, {"field": "delivered_at"}]
}
```

How the pieces fit:

- **Local Saturdays out, in Pulse.** `FEAT_DATE_FEATURES` adds `local_dow`, the ISO day of week (Monday = 1, Saturday = 6) of each row's *local* day, read through `time_zone_field` like the grouper. `FILTER_EXCLUDE` on `local_dow = 6` then drops Sydney's Saturdays on Sydney days and Los Angeles's on Los Angeles days. A UTC weekday would drop the wrong rows at the four sites whose working hours cross UTC midnight (LAX, ORD, NYC and SYD). Filters can see features but not attributes, so the day of week has to be a feature here.
- **One series per warehouse.** With two groupers, the series overlays partition by every key except the last (here `warehouse`) and run down the last key (the day), so the date grouper goes last. Each warehouse's rolling window starts fresh. Predict raises `PULSE_ADVISORY_SERIES_AXIS_NOT_DATE` when the order is wrong.
- **`min_periods: 28`.** This is the default, written out because it is a decision. A day gets a score only when all 28 prior operating days exist. Each site's first 28 weekdays (to Wed 2026-03-11) therefore carry `null`, and scoring starts on Thu 2026-03-12. The z-score divides by the window's standard deviation, which is unreliable when it is estimated from a handful of days. With `min_periods: 5`, the same request adds two early false alarms, LON Fri 02-27 (z −3.31) and ORD Mon 03-09 (z −3.23), both scored against less than six weeks of history. `min_periods: 10` adds the same two. A full window costs about six weeks of coverage at the start of a series and changes nothing after that.

- Over 6 × 130 warehouse-days, the planted outage gives the strongest signal in the network: z = −7.39 and an index of 46 on its first day, meaning the day ran at 46% of its own recent norm. The three outage days have the three lowest rolling indexes (30–49). The next lowest is 72.6.

### Step 4: Real trend or noise?

**Request.** [`req/08_weekly_on_time_trend.json`](req/08_weekly_on_time_trend.json) groups by warehouse and local ISO week (`GROUP_DATE` `component: week`, read through `time_zone_field`) and runs a Mann-Kendall test on each warehouse's 26 weekly on-time rates:

```json
{
  "cohort": {"filename": "deliveries.pulse", "data_dir": "."},
  "time_zone_field": "zone",
  "multiplicity": {"method": "holm", "family": "request"},
  "groups": [
    {"type": "GROUP_CATEGORY", "field": "warehouse"},
    {"type": "GROUP_DATE", "field": "delivered_at", "params": {"component": "week"}}
  ],
  "aggregations": [
    {"type": "AGG_AVERAGE", "field": "on_time", "label": "on_time_rate"},
    {"type": "AGG_COUNT", "field": "delivery_id", "label": "deliveries"}
  ],
  "post_tests": [
    {"type": "TEST_TREND", "field": "on_time_rate", "label": "on_time_trend", "alpha": 0.05,
     "order_by": [{"field": "delivered_at"}], "params": {"variant": "mann_kendall"}}
  ],
  "sort": [{"field": "warehouse"}, {"field": "delivered_at"}]
}
```

With two groupers, a post-test runs once per partition: once for each value of every key but the last, so once per warehouse. Each result carries its `group_key` (`["BLR"]`). The `multiplicity` block's `request` family pools the six trend p-values into one Holm correction (`m = 6`). That is the same family the earlier six-slot batch formed with `family: compose`, and the Z, p and Holm-adjusted p of every site are identical to it.

**Result.**

| warehouse | on-time W06 | on-time W31 | Mann-Kendall Z | Kendall τ | p | Holm-adjusted p | trend? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **BLR** | 78.1% | 95.1% | **3.79** | 0.532 | 1.5 × 10⁻⁴ | **9.0 × 10⁻⁴** | **yes, improving** |
| LAX | 88.7% | 95.1% | 1.15 | 0.164 | 0.25 | 1.00 | no |
| LON | 93.9% | 83.3% | −0.13 | −0.022 | 0.89 | 1.00 | no |
| NYC | 86.9% | 91.4% | 1.54 | 0.218 | 0.12 | 0.61 | no |
| ORD | 92.8% | 94.6% | 1.12 | 0.160 | 0.26 | 1.00 | no |
| SYD | 91.6% | 94.2% | 0.97 | 0.138 | 0.33 | 1.00 | no |

**Reading.**

- Bengaluru's improvement is real and strong (τ = 0.53).
- London's first-to-last swing (93.9% → 83.3%) is noise, and so are the swings at the other four sites. Comparing first and last week would have "found" a 10-point decline at LON and improvements at LAX, NYC, ORD and SYD.
- The SYD outage week does not create a trend, because Mann-Kendall is rank-based.

**Request: don't test a smoothed series.** [`req/09_daily_trend_raw_vs_smoothed.json`](req/09_daily_trend_raw_vs_smoothed.json) runs the same test on 156 daily values, raw and after a 6-day `WIN_MOVING_AVG`.

**Result.**

| series | BLR Z | BLR p | LON Z | LON p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| daily raw | 4.83 | 1.3 × 10⁻⁶ | 0.08 | 0.93 |
| daily 6-day moving average | 9.89 | 4.7 × 10⁻²³ | 0.11 | 0.91 |

**Reading.** Smoothing makes neighbouring points dependent, which inflates the statistic. BLR's Z doubles from 4.83 to 9.89 and its p-value falls by 17 orders of magnitude, with no new information. LON has no trend to inflate, so its Z barely moves. Test weekly aggregates or raw daily values, and keep moving averages for charts.

**Reading.**

- On local wall-clock time Pulse never loses or double-counts a batch:
  - the repeated 02:00 hour is one bucket holding both passes (8 batches);
  - the skipped 02:00 hour has no bucket;
  - every night's total is preserved.
- The UTC rows expose what a naive feed has lost. Sydney's two passes through 02:00 were really an hour apart (15Z and 16Z). The feed's strings can't tell them apart, so either policy stacks all 8 into one UTC hour and leaves the other empty.
- For New York's four impossible stamps, `earlier` reads them as EST and lands them in 03:xx EDT. `later` reads them as EDT and lands them in 01:xx EST.

## Findings and implications

1. **Report every warehouse on its own local calendar.** On UTC days the network shows 289 Sunday deliveries at sites that are closed on Sundays. Sydney's Saturday shows 157 deliveries instead of 254, and Los Angeles's Monday 412 instead of 518.
2. **Incidents land on the wrong day in UTC.** Sydney's Tue–Thu outage reads in UTC as Mon–Wed with a "partial recovery" on Thursday. The UTC view shows on-time falling from 90.1% to 52.1%, when on Sydney days it fell from 94.2% to 38.9%.
3. **Rolling overlays catch the outage, but only on local days.**
   - On Sydney days the outage is the strongest signal across 6 warehouses × 130 local weekdays: on-time z = −7.39 and index 46 on its first day, and its three days hold the three lowest indexes (30–49). In SYD's own daily series, duration reaches z = 9.61 and index 171.
   - On UTC days the same screen fires on the wrong weekday, misses the worst day (z −1.70) and raises a false alarm on a 3-delivery fragment.
   - Saturday volumes (about 10 parcels) caused three false alarms. Excluding local Saturdays inside the request (`FEAT_DATE_FEATURES` `dow` + `FILTER_EXCLUDE`, each row on its own calendar) removes them and leaves six weekday false alarms, all at index 72.6 or above. Pair the z-score with the rolling index before paging anyone.
4. **Only Bengaluru's improvement is a real trend** (Mann-Kendall Z = 3.79, Holm-adjusted p = 9.0 × 10⁻⁴; 78.1% → 95.1%). London and the other four sites also move between their first and last weeks (London by −10.6 points), but none of those moves is significant (adjusted p ≥ 0.61). Test weekly or raw daily values, not a moving average, which inflates Z from 4.83 to 9.89.
5. **Build staffing rosters in local hours.** Each site's peak is a fixed local hour: SYD 08:00, ORD 09:00, NYC 10:00, LON 14:00, LAX 15:00, and BLR has two equal peaks at 10:00 and 18:00. In UTC that peak moves by an hour at each DST change, at a different date per hemisphere.
6. **Naive-time feeds need a DST policy and should be fixed at source.** Pulse refuses 8 ambiguous Sydney stamps and 4 impossible New York stamps by default. It resolves them only when told how, and counts what it changed. Local nightly totals stay exact (28 batches on Sydney's 25-hour night), but the order inside the repeated hour cannot be recovered without offsets.
